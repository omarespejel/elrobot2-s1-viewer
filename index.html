Design versions

A printable arm on a vertical column that reaches through the rear of a cooler and puts cans and PET bottles onto shelf lanes. Status as of 4 October 2026.

Status. Design, simulation and desk research only. Nothing has been printed, built or measured. The rack dimensions used here are inferred from public catalogues of US manufacturers and Mexican resellers; they are not measurements of any store.

Versions

Design of record · CAD and simulation · not built

S1 v1.1: Z-lift SCARA with a top-down gripper

  • Two 200 mm links on vertical axes (shoulder and elbow STS3095 servos, wrist and gripper STS3215) on a 4040 V-slot column; carriage travel 0.37–1.06 m.
  • Rack-and-pinion gripper with 140 mm fingers and TPU pads; PETG parts for a desktop printer.
  • Simulation on the test fixture: 45 of 45 accepted picks dry and 45 of 45 wet; 600 mL Monte Carlo 60/60 at both shelf levels; 1 L at level 1 only.

Limit. The gripper stack stands 140 mm above the object, so it needs a shelf pitch of about 0.41–0.43 m for a 600 mL bottle. Standard racks are 0.287–0.381 m (inferred): S1 serves cans, not 600 mL or larger PET bottles.

S2-RC2: neck-grip collar jaws on the S1 arm

  • Keeps the column, base, joints and links of S1. The hand is replaced by two printed collar jaws that close on the cap and carry the bottle by a lip under the support ring (the place where public photographs show the Telexistence hand holding bottles).
  • Needs a shelf pitch of 0.277 m for a 600 mL bottle (S1: 0.417 m), so it fits the 0.287, 0.318 and 0.381 m racks. 500 and 600 mL PET are verified; 1 L is conditional; cans are not supported in the lanes.
  • Exact-mesh checks: 576 of 576 placement poses, 792 of 792 release and retreat poses, 42 of 42 tote poses and 65 of 65 seating cases are free of interference. MuJoCo Monte Carlo on a stand-in bench with an assumed neck: type C places a 500 mL bottle in 94 % and a 600 mL bottle in 72 % of runs at ±4 mm lateral error, and 99 % of both when the lateral error is centred to ±1.5 mm.

Limits. The bottle neck is assumed and has not been measured (Gate 0). The printed S1 wrist chain cannot carry a bottle (safety factor 0.28); a metal kit replaces it. The lane leaves only (73 − D)/2 of slack per side. Droop of the jaws meets its limit only with PTFE shims. Nothing has been printed or tested on hardware.

Stage 0 · existing arm

AM-ARM200 (open-source arm)

  • Used for teleoperated demonstrations and data collection with cans and 500 mL bottles.
  • Not the production body: with a 600 mL bottle its most-stressed joint loads 0.95–2.6 times rated torque (calculation, report Section 2).

Why a second version

Catalogues give five, six or seven shelves in a 75–79 in rack door, which is a shelf pitch of 0.287–0.381 m when the opening height is divided by the shelf count (0.318 m is the design-driving case). The smallest pitch at which each end effector fits above each object comes from a clearance model, pmin = H + T + margin + R, with 30 mm of shelf structure T and a 5 mm margin; it reproduces the S1 planner's own minima to within 0.01–0.02 m. R is the height of end-effector structure above the object top (S1 140 mm, S2 flat clamp 50 mm, S2 neck-jaw 15 mm, push-in plate 0 mm). R for the 2 October S2 concepts is a target; the S2-RC2 column is computed from the CAD (R = 0).

What is assumed or unknown

  • Nothing has been built or measured. The simulation is a design check, not a prediction of hardware success rates.
  • The shelf pitch is inferred from shelf counts and opening heights; shelves are adjustable and no store was measured. The structure thickness (30 mm) is a fitted constant and the PET support-ring diameter (33 mm) is an assumption.
  • Published friction values for printed TPU are all dry; the wet design value of 0.3 is an inference. A 1 L bottle needs cast pads or form closure at the neck.
  • S2-RC2 is untested on hardware. The bottle neck (cap, support ring, free neck under the ring) is assumed from the PCO-1881 finish and must be measured first (Gate 0); a free neck below 3 mm would force a redesign of the lip.
  • Placement tolerance is the limit in the simulation: the 73 mm lane leaves (73 − D)/2 of slack per side, and a 600 mL bottle succeeds in 72 % of runs at ±4 mm lateral error and 99 % at ±1.5 mm. Friction values are assumed, not measured; the stand-in bench is not a real rack.
  • S2-RC2 does not place cans in the lanes (the retreat is blocked), and 1.5 L and 2 L bottles are not supported; 1 L is conditional.
  • Scope: aluminium cans and one-way PET bottles. Glass and returnable containers are out of scope.
